# Intake Checklist

Use this only when the user's starting point is scattered, underspecified, or overloaded with too many possible directions.

## Minimum Inputs

Try to pin down at least these seven items:

- topic or domain
- motivating puzzle
- unit of analysis
- main outcome
- candidate comparison, shock, or source of variation
- possible data source
- practical constraints

## Best Follow-Up Questions

Ask only the questions that unblock the next narrowing step.

- What concrete fact, pattern, or anecdote made you think this is a project?
- What is the exact unit of analysis: person, firm, school, region, market, paper, or something else?
- What is the most important outcome you want on the left-hand side?
- What changes across units or over time in a way that might identify an effect?
- What data source could realistically measure the outcome and the comparison?
- Which paper is the closest template, even if you do not want to copy it?
- What constraint matters most: time, data access, coding burden, institutional detail, or econometric difficulty?

## Fast Diagnostic Moves

- If the user gives a topic but no puzzle, ask for one motivating fact.
- If the user gives a puzzle but no outcome, ask what exact variable would appear in the first figure or table.
- If the user gives an outcome but no comparison, ask what would plausibly vary across units, time, or institutions.
- If the user gives a comparison but no data, ask where each main variable would come from.
- If the user gives many ideas at once, rank them by feasibility and keep one as the lead idea.

## Red Flags

Slow down and push back when the idea depends on any of these:

- no observable unit of analysis
- no named outcome
- no plausible data access path
- a causal claim with no comparison or variation
- a mechanism story with no way to test or proxy the channel
- multiple papers being forced into one proposal

## If The Idea Is Still Too Early

Do not fake precision. Produce a short scoping memo with:

- the best current skeleton
- the top missing pieces
- the next 3 to 5 things to verify
- the most promising fallback path
