01

Core Idea And Contribution

This project studies whether [institutional or market shock] changes [operational, behavioral, political, or economic outcome] for [unit of analysis]. The proposal is built around a concrete setting, a clearly observed source of variation, and a comparison that can separate the main explanation from plausible alternatives.

Question
How does [X] affect [Y] for [unit] in [setting]?
Contribution
The project contributes by connecting a previously under-measured institutional shock to measurable outcomes, mechanisms, and decision-relevant consequences.
Design
The main design compares exposed and less-exposed units before and after the shock, with attention to local comparability, timing, and alternative explanations.
Main Risk
The key risk is that exposure may correlate with pre-existing geography, selection, or strategic assignment rather than the shock itself.
Extension
Add field-specific theory, institutional detail, data construction, measurement validation, qualitative evidence, ethics, simulation, or appendix material only where it strengthens the proposal's logic.

Motivation, Puzzle, And Benchmark

Motivating Problem

The project starts from a tension: [expected pattern] does not fully explain [observed pattern]. The setting matters because the units are exposed to a change in rules, representation, markets, technology, or organization without necessarily changing their underlying location or identity.

Benchmark Literature

The closest benchmark is [paper, theory, method, policy debate, or managerial decision]. That benchmark establishes [known relationship], but leaves open whether the same logic holds for [new unit, mechanism, setting, or outcome].

Contribution Claim

The contribution is not that every unit is affected. The sharper claim is that the shock matters for units with [exposure condition], because it changes [relationship, constraint, risk, incentive, information, or access channel].

Research Questions And Hypotheses

  1. RQ1
    Does [X] change the primary outcome [Y]?

    Hypothesis: exposed units experience [directional change] in [Y] relative to comparable units, especially where exposure is stronger or more decision-relevant.

  2. RQ2
    Which mechanism links the shock to the outcome?

    Hypothesis: the effect operates through [relationship disruption, uncertainty, access, information, coordination, investment, or supply-chain adjustment], and should be visible in mechanism-specific outcomes.

  3. RQ3
    Which units respond most strongly?

    Hypothesis: effects are concentrated among units with high dependence on the affected institution, high adjustment costs, high public exposure, or low ability to diversify.

Source Unit Key Fields Use In Design Main Limitation

Linking Design

The core file links [unit] to [exposure source] using [location, identifier, name, time, network relation, or merge key]. The proposal should distinguish units whose underlying location or identity is stable from units that move, enter, exit, or change composition.

06

Treatment Definitions And Controls

A strong proposal separates simple exposure from mechanism-specific shocks. It also states what untreated or less-treated units represent.

Treatment Definition Interpretation Best Control
Baseline exposure Unit becomes newly exposed because [boundary, rule, assignment, or eligibility] changes. Captures the average effect of institutional reassignment. Stable units in the same local market, pre-period group, industry, or risk set.
Attribute shock Exposure changes along party, seniority, committee, intensity, quality, price, access, or compatibility. Tests a more specific mechanism. Units with exposure changes but not the same attribute change.
Unexpected assignment Actual exposure differs from the assignment expected under feasible alternatives or propensity scores. Reduces concern that exposure simply reflects geography or selection. Units with similar predicted exposure under counterfactual assignments.

Identification And Empirical Strategy

The identification claim should be modest and explicit: conditional on geography, fixed traits, pre-trends, and institutional constraints, exposed and comparison units would have followed similar outcome paths absent the shock.

Design Purpose What Must Be Shown
Event study Trace dynamics before and after the shock. No strong pre-trends and effects begin after the exposure becomes active.
Difference-in-differences Estimate the baseline average change. Comparable controls, defensible fixed effects, and stable sample construction.
Boundary, threshold, or local comparison Compare nearby or nearly eligible units. Results survive narrow windows and local fixed effects.
Counterfactual assignment or machine learning adjustment Model assignment propensity and heterogeneity. Used as support, not as the only source of identification.

Scope Of Claim

If the comparison is descriptive, the proposal should make a descriptive claim. If the variation supports causal interpretation, the proposal should name the identifying assumption and the strongest reason a reviewer might reject it.

08

Outcomes, Mechanisms, And Heterogeneity

Layer Measures Reason To Include
Main outcomes Growth, performance, survival, adoption, capacity, behavior, quality, or movement. Shows whether the shock changes the primary object of interest.
Mechanisms Information search, disclosure, contracts, network links, customer or supplier concentration, investment, or risk management. Distinguishes the proposed channel from a reduced-form pattern.
Heterogeneity Exposure intensity, dependence, regulation, geography, size, constraints, public visibility, or cluster position. Tests whether the effect appears where the theory predicts it should.
09

Validity Threats And Checks

The proposal should not hide the strongest identification threats. It should convert them into planned tests, restrictions, and alternative interpretations.

Threat Why It Matters Planned Response
Strategic assignment or sorting Exposure may be correlated with unit value, geography, or expected outcomes. Use local comparisons, simulated or predicted assignment, rich pre-period covariates, and sensitivity checks.
Measurement error Misclassified location, exposure, timing, or outcomes can attenuate or bias results. Audit links, remove uncertain boundary cases, validate aggregates, and test alternative definitions.
Local economic trends Treated units may sit in changing local markets. Use local-year fixed effects, boundary windows, pre-trends, and placebo years.
Weak treatment for many units The average effect may be small even if the mechanism is real for exposed subgroups. Pre-specify politically, institutionally, or operationally exposed subgroups.
10

Interpretation Tree

Positive
If the predicted effect appears, the project supports a claim about [mechanism] under [scope condition], not a universal claim for all units.
Null
If estimates are small or noisy, the project can still clarify whether the setting lacks power, the treatment is weak, the mechanism is rare, or the theory needs narrowing.
Mixed
If results vary by subgroup or outcome, the paper pivots toward exposure intensity, mechanism-specific responses, or limits of external validity.
11

Execution Roadmap

  1. Build the unit-level panel and verify stable units, identifiers, timing, and exclusions.
  2. Construct treatment variants, mechanism variables, and predicted exposure or counterfactual assignment measures.
  3. Produce the first descriptive table and first visualization that prove the design exists at meaningful scale.
  4. Estimate event-study and local-comparison specifications with transparent validity checks.
  5. Decide whether the project should continue as the full causal paper, a measurement paper, a mechanism paper, or a descriptive setting paper.
